The Flag

At the heart of the blue canton are the red tower and gold eagle of Castellammare del Golfo.

The field carries nine alternating red-and-yellow stripes, beginning and ending with red, representing Sicily’s nine historic provinces: Palermo, Messina, Catania, Agrigento, Trapani, Siracusa, Enna, Caltanissetta, and Ragusa. The striped field and canton preserve an American visual grammar, while the heraldry and color palette point back to Sicily.

The colors are grounded in Sicily’s official symbols. The stripes follow the same two colors specified for the official Sicilian regional flag: rosso aranciato and giallo. The canton blue is grounded in the azzurro used in Sicilian heraldry and in the regional gonfalone.

Production color standard: Pantone 2945 C blue (working match for the canton), Pantone 1795 C red, Pantone 116 C yellow/gold, and Pantone Black C for outlines. The on-screen HEX values are optimized web approximations of those production colors.

Sicilian Catholic Heritage

A small gold cross in the tower doorway represents Sicily’s Catholic heritage.

Sicily’s Christian history reaches back to the apostolic age. Acts 28:12 records St. Paul stopping at Syracuse for three days on his journey to Rome. That ancient Christian presence became part of the island’s enduring Catholic tradition.

In Castellammare del Golfo, that heritage continues in the long tradition of the parish of Maria SS. del Soccorso, canonically erected in 1526 and commemorated in its 500th anniversary year in 2026.

Historical references: Italian Government heraldic record · Diocese of Trapani · Comune di Castellammare del Golfo

Three Eagle Styles

The eagle has appeared in many cultures over centuries. In Sicily, it became part of local civic and imperial heraldry; in America, it became a national symbol. This flag draws on both traditions, but the eagle shown here is an original design created specifically for the Castellammarese-American flag.
